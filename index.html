DESIGN DOSSIER / CONCEPT STAGE

A study in
vertical geometry.

The Isosceles concept is presented as an exploration of high-rise form, stacked floor space and a pronounced relationship between enclosure, circulation and the ground.

The model expresses a slender tower with repeated vertical façade elements, stepped upper volumes and a distinct crown. A broader, curved ground-level composition anchors the tower above a substantial below-grade volume. The viewer retains every source mesh; the presentation palette is a visual interpretation, with original materials available.

Architectural development

Use the parallel elevations to assess silhouette, floor repetition and façade rhythm. Use a horizontal cut to review floor geometry and circulation; use vertical cuts to study stairs, stacked volumes and the transition below grade.

Programme, occupancy, site orientation and a verified storey schedule were not supplied. Assign these before assessing usable area, accessibility, escape routes, servicing or façade performance.

Structural integrity / review basis

The file is a geometric concept, not an analytical model. Material labels identify “structure walls” and “foundation wall”, but they do not establish load paths, reinforcement, stiffness, connection design or capacity.

The explicitly labelled structural-wall group occupies source Y −20 to −5, while the foundation-wall group extends from −32.60 to −3. These labels alone therefore do not identify a complete above-grade structural system.

A candidate scheme would transfer floor loads through verified vertical supports into a designed foundation system. A continuous lateral-resisting system and diaphragms would need to be defined and checked for wind, seismic response, torsion and drift.

No safe load, member size, foundation bearing capacity, safety factor or code-compliance conclusion can be derived from this GLB alone.

Priority design questions
  1. Verify survey, model units, site grade and the intended floor schedule.
  2. Map floor support lines and any setbacks, overhangs or transfer conditions.
  3. Confirm continuous gravity and lateral load paths across every level.
  4. Resolve below-grade walls, waterproofing, groundwater and soil constraints.
  5. Coordinate protected egress, accessible routes, vertical services and façade movement joints.

Engineering handoff

Supply a surveyed site, geotechnical report, occupancy and loading schedule, material properties, member sections, connections, reinforcement, foundation design and the applicable local code basis.

The next stage is a coordinated BIM and structural analytical model, followed by strength, stability, serviceability, dynamic response and construction-sequence review by the project engineer.

Coordinate & scale basis

glTF uses a right-handed coordinate system with Y up and a metre convention. The author’s real-world scale is not independently verified. Values default to model units; only enable metres after calibration.

Camera accuracy

Perspective FOV is vertical. Horizontal FOV is calculated from the actual render aspect ratio: 2 atan(tan(vFOV / 2) × aspect). Lens equivalence uses a fixed 36 mm sensor width.

Parallel views eliminate perspective convergence. Orthographic scale is shown as model units per screen pixel; physical 1:100 or 1:200 print scale cannot be guaranteed by a browser screenshot.
